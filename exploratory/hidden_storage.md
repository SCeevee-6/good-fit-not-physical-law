# Identical port physics, opposite internal energy flow

## What is observable?

Voltage and current at an ideal one-port determine the supplied power P=vi. Determining how much enters electromagnetic storage rather than dissipation is a different inverse problem. All devices below obey the same energy balance. Their physical inductor, capacitor or electromagnetic field energy is specified by construction.

### A four-component witness

Put a series R-L branch in parallel with a series R-C branch, using equal resistors R, L=Rτ and C=τ/R. Start with zero stored energy. If x is the capacitor voltage, both x and Ri_L satisfy τx'=v−x with initial value zero. The total current is therefore x/R+(v−x)/R=v/R, for every drive. The physical stored energy is E=τx²/R; its derivative is 2x(v−x)/R. Thus every τ has precisely the same complete driving-point map, even for adaptive choices of a well-posed drive. Randomization cannot distinguish two hypotheses whose terminal observations have identical distributions.

For the prescribed 2-to-1 V step at t=1 after starting from rest, x(1)=2(1−exp(−1/τ)). For R=1, τ=0.2 and 3 yield rates −3.9195 and +0.4910 W immediately after the step. Both accept +1 W. The first simultaneously dissipates the incoming watt and released energy, while the second stores part of the incoming watt. A single high-impedance observation of capacitor voltage at that instant settles the sign, assuming the specified circuit topology. With 1% capacitor mismatch, the sign survives in both examples, but perfect port equivalence disappears. The tested waveform yields deviations of 0.366% and 0.237% of peak ideal current; these figures do not bound all possible drives.

### Exact feasible histories for a matched delay bank

Fix terminal resistance R and a finite list of observation times t_j. Use initially quiescent, lossless, nondispersive matched lines in parallel with one-way delays d in a compact set D. Give branch i characteristic and load resistance R/w_i, positive weights summing to one. Define P(t)=v(t)²/R and take zero prehistory or account for the full initial traveling-wave history. Every such bank has exactly the same terminal law. A single branch takes power w_i P(t) at the port and dissipates w_i P(t−d_i) at its termination. Conservation gives total field energy E(t)=Σ_i w_i ∫ from t−d_i to t of P(s)ds.

**Theorem.** For continuous P the attainable vector of storage rates at the selected times is precisely the convex hull of the delay curve d ↦ (P(t_j)−P(t_j−d))_j. Each vector in k dimensions is realizable with at most k+1 branches. Proof: energy balance gives the convex combination; instantiate its weights as branch impedances for the converse and apply Carathéodory's theorem. Compactness closes the hull. In one dimension the range is [P(t)−max_D P(t−d), P(t)−min_D P(t−d)]. If the endpoints straddle zero there are genuine opposite-sign systems that every terminal-only probing policy fails to distinguish. The joint hull also excludes impossible combinations that independent pointwise intervals can admit: one physical bank must use the same weights at every time. Sharpness here is for this realization family, not for all passive devices.

### Which extra measurement restores identification?

Apply a unit port-power step from rest and monitor the sum of the powers dissipated at the terminating loads. This can be computed from load-side electrical measurements; an actual thermal sensor has its own response function. At time s the aggregate normalized load power equals Σ w_i 1[d_i≤s], the delay distribution's cumulative distribution function. Thus one complete step-response trace identifies the functional for every future drive without identifying individual branches of equal delay. With only bin masses m_b from the step, the sharp closure of the single-time feasible range has endpoints P(t) minus the weighted sum of within-bin suprema and infima, respectively. Proof: move each bin's entire mass arbitrarily close to its extremizing delay. A known Lipschitz bound K on P limits the interval width to K times the mass-weighted bin width. The simulation uses a padded numerical enclosure and assumes exact calibration samples. It does not reconstruct a hidden network from noisy heat measurements.

### Numerical and experimental status

Run OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python exploratory/hidden_storage.py. See hidden_components.csv and hidden_delay.csv. The delay-bank check uses an 80-branch synthetic bank and a smooth two-frequency waveform after calibration; its prehistory over the relevant interval is assumed known. All 701 rate values were enclosed. Increasing calibration from four to 32 bins reduced mean enclosure width from 0.856 to 0.123 in normalized power units. Four bins certified a sign at 58.8% of times; 32 certified 96.4%. The one-bin conservative enclosure certified none. Those percentages describe this waveform and bank.

Matched lines, constant-resistance networks, energy balance, convex hulls and step-response identification are established engineering and mathematical tools. The worked inverse problem connects them. No world-first claim. A decisive bench study would hide which time constant is installed, predeclare a drive and sign prediction, measure terminal mismatch against independently calibrated precision, and use internal capacitor and inductor channels for truth. Time constants can be rescaled together with the pulse duration; component tolerances, winding loss, parasitic impedances and sensor loading must be measured. The strong falsification test is whether a terminal-only classifier can exploit such leakage under blinded repeats or alternative drives. Physical validation and comprehensive novelty review are pending.
