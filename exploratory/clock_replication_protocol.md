# External replication of tail-calibrated local sign resolution

Frozen after the cesium result and before scoring the GPS or cable-delay records. Same chronological thirds, 128-sample blocks, last h increments, h=4,16,64, linear ramp and trend as clock_protocol.md. Test three designs: original model (Gaussian amplitude), same detector with calibration-tail amplitude and offset, and nonnegative detector fitted with 80% empirical increment covariance plus 20% modeled covariance and the same tail calibration. First third fixes all detector weights. Second third fixes midpoint threshold and amplitude from the 10th and 90th order statistics, expanded using a binomial 97.5% one-sided tolerance bound per tail. No final-third fitting. This chooses a predicted minimum terminal frequency offset for 90% per sign under the measured noise. For iid blocks the two-tail construction has at least 95% simultaneous confidence for a fixed detector; dependence in clock records makes it an empirical transfer test. For rare ties add tiny floating-point slack. Primary gate: for the original detector with tail calibration, worst-sign held-out accuracy >=87% in every external record/window. Report margin cost relative to Gaussian-only design, plus the enhanced detector. No pooling of signs as independent observations. Include the previously seen cesium record as developmental. GPS sample indices are taken as nominal one-second 1PPS samples; no timestamp gap information available.
